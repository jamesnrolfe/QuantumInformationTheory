As a final section, we will consider some of the basic aspects of quantum information science that will underly a potential future **quantum internet**. 

But what is the quantum internet? Much like the classical internet, the basic idea is that one day, there will be a global network scale of nodes, able to exchange *quantum communication* and *quantum entanglement*. 

Let's start with some of the topics covered in this course. We have seen that if two parties share *quantum entanglement*, then via the process of [[4.4 Teleportation|quantum teleportation]] we can send quantum states using only classical communication. On the one hand, this shows that *if* we are able *distribute entanglement* around the globe, then this could be used to facilitate quantum communication. On the other hand, this is furthermore a desirable way to communicate quantum information. In reality, quantum communication will *always* be noisy, whereas classical information is effectively noise-free. 

What might be the use of such quantum information? There are many possibilities that could be mentioned. First, it is conceivable that the first **quantum computers** will be in the *cloud* (due to their size and expense). It is then imaginable that users will send jobs to the computer, and might want to keep some level of privacy. Being able to send qubits to a quantum computer, encoding data, and receiving qubits back is a use-case that might arise.

Another use-case might be in cryptography. We have already seen [[4.8 Quantum cryptography|quantum key distribution]]. 

Key distribution is only the tip of the iceberg. Ideas such as **digital signatures** and **secret sharing** are active areas of research. 

As said above, at the core of the quantum internet will be **entanglement distribution**. In particular, there are two challenges to address. 

The first of these is the need to distribute entanglement over long distances. This is an issue because of the aforementioned noise, and qubits essentially get lost over global scales. A solution to this is [[12.1 Entanglement swapping and quantum repeaters|entanglement swapping]], which is a generalisation of [[4.4 Teleportation|teleportation]]. 

Second, we need high-quality qubits. In reality, we can never distribute *perfect* qubits. We can use a process called [[12.2 Entanglement purification|entanglement purification]] to take a large number of low-quality qubits, and turn them into a small number of high-quality qubits.
