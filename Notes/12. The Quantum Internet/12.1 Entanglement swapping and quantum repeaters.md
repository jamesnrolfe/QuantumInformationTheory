#Notes 

>[!summary] Summary
>What this shows is that if Alice teleports a qubit which is entangled to another qubit, then by following the standard teleportation procedure:
>- Alice measures her qubit with an operator that has eigenstates equal to the Bell states
>- Alice communicates the result of the measurement to Bob
>- Bob corrects the state based on this result by applying the appropriate Pauli operator, corresponding to Alice's result
>
>then Alice and Bob manage to *swap entanglement* such that Bob and Charlie end up in the exact same entangled state initially shared by Alice and Charlie, without Bob and Charlie's qubits ever physically interacting.
>This allows Charlie and Bob to use Alice as a *repeater*, essentially allowing them to transmit entanglement over longer distances than they otherwise would have been able to.

Let's consider the following question. 

>*What happens if Alice tries to teleport to Bob half of an entangled state?*

More precisely, imagine that Alice shares the entangled state
$$
|\Phi \rangle =\alpha|0\rangle |0\rangle +\beta|1\rangle |1\rangle 
$$
with Charlie, such that Alice holds one qubit, and Charlie holds the other (and we assume that $\alpha \not\in \{ 0,1 \}$). Alice now decides that she would like to teleport her qubit to Bob, with whom she also shares the maximally entangled state, as in the standard [[4.4 Teleportation|teleportation]] program. What happens?

There are a number of initial reactions that one might have. For example, we could consider that Alice teleports her [[7.1 The reduced density operator|reduced density operator]] $\rho_{A}$ to Bob. We might imagine that it only works if Alice is [[7.2 Identifying entanglement using the reduced density operator#^039366|unentangled]] from Charlie.

It turns our that teleportation works *just as before*, and *maintains the entanglement with Charlie*! That is, Alice really teleports her qubit, and this qubit retains the entanglement it has with Charlie's qubit, such that at the end of the protocol, Bob and Charlie's qubits have become entangled! This is remarkable, since Bob and Charlie's qubits *have never interacted*, but nevertheless, we have entangled them *at a distance*. 

To see this, we can start in a similar fashion to how we started with [[4.4 Teleportation|teleportation]]. The initial state, with the qubits labelled, is 
$$
|\Psi\rangle =(\alpha|0\rangle _{C}|0\rangle _{A}+\beta|1\rangle _{C}|1\rangle _{A}) \frac{|0\rangle _{A'}|0\rangle _{B}+|1\rangle _{A'}|1\rangle _{B}}{\sqrt{ 2 }}
$$
where we have labelled the two qubits held by Alice as $A$ and $A'$ respectively. 

It will be useful to do two things that this stage. 
1. The ordering of the qubits makes it difficult to see what is going on, so we need to write them in a different order. There is no physical change here. We can use the order $A,A',B,C$. In this system, we write
$$|\Psi\rangle =\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{ 2 }}|0\rangle _{A}|0\rangle _{A'}|0\rangle _{B}|0\rangle _{C}+ \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{ 2 }}|0\rangle _{A}|1\rangle _{A'}|1\rangle _{B}|0\rangle _{C}+ \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{ 2 }}|1\rangle _{A}|0\rangle _{A'}|0\rangle _{B}|1\rangle _{C}+ \frac{\beta}{\sqrt{ 2 }}|1\rangle _{A}|1\rangle _{A'}|1\rangle _{B}|1\rangle _{C}$$
2. Similar to how we analysed teleportation, it is useful now to rewrite Alice's two qubits in the basis of the [[Vectors#^73d0e2|Bell states]]. We can see that  
$$\begin{aligned} |\Phi_{I}\rangle &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(|00\rangle+|11\rangle) \\|\Phi_{Z}\rangle &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(|00\rangle-|11\rangle) \\|\Psi_{X}\rangle &=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(|01\rangle + |10\rangle)\\|\Psi_{Y}\rangle&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(|01\rangle-|10\rangle)\end{aligned}$$
and so 
$$
\begin{align}
|0\rangle |0\rangle  & = \frac{|\Phi_{I}\rangle +|\Phi_{Z}\rangle }{\sqrt{ 2 }} \\
|0\rangle |1\rangle  & = \frac{|\Phi_{X}\rangle +|\Phi_{Y}\rangle }{\sqrt{ 2 }} \\
|1\rangle |0\rangle  & = \frac{|\Phi_{X}\rangle -|\Phi_{Y}\rangle }{\sqrt{ 2 }} \\
|1\rangle |1\rangle  & = \frac{|\Phi_{I}\rangle -|\Phi_{Z}\rangle }{\sqrt{ 2 }}
\end{align}
$$

Then, substituting these in for Alice's two qubits, we can see that
$$
\begin{gathered}
|\Psi\rangle = \frac{\alpha}{2}(|\Phi_{I}\rangle _{AA'}+|\Phi_{Z}\rangle _{AA'})|0\rangle _{B}|0\rangle _{C}+ \frac{\alpha}{2}(|\Phi_{X}\rangle _{AA'}+|\Phi_{Y}\rangle _{AA'})|1\rangle _{B}|0\rangle _{C} 
\\+ \frac{\beta}{2}(|\Phi_{X}\rangle _{AA'}-|\Phi_{Y}\rangle _{AA'})|0\rangle _{B}|1\rangle _{C} +\frac{\beta}{2}(|\Phi_{I}\rangle _{AA'}-|\Phi_{Z}\rangle _{AA'})|1\rangle _{B}|1\rangle _{C} 
\end{gathered}

$$
Collecting together the states in Alice's qubits, this is further equal to
$$
\begin{gathered}
|\Psi\rangle =\frac{1}{2} |\Phi_{I}\rangle_{AA'} (\alpha|0\rangle_{B}|0\rangle_{C}+\beta|1\rangle_{B}|1\rangle_{C})+ \frac{1}{2}|\Phi_{X}\rangle_{AA'}(\alpha|1\rangle _{B}  |0\rangle _{C}+\beta|0\rangle _{B}|1\rangle _{C}) \\
+ \frac{1}{2} |\Phi_{Y}\rangle _{AA'}(\alpha|1\rangle _{B}|0\rangle _{C}-\beta|0\rangle _{B}|1\rangle _{C})+\frac{1}{2}|\Phi_{Z}\rangle_{AA'} (\alpha|0\rangle _{B}|0\rangle _{C}-|1\rangle _{B}|1\rangle _{C})
\end{gathered}
$$
When written out like this, we can see that the state is a superposition of Alice's qubits being in one of the four Bell states. 

As in teleportation, Alice now measures an operator which has the four Bell states as eigenstates, with corresponding eigenvalues $\lambda_{I},\lambda_{X},\lambda_{Y},\lambda_{Z}$. Given the form of the state, we can read off the result, and each eigenvalue will be obtained with probability $| \frac{1}{2}|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}$. If Alice obtains, for example, $\lambda_{I}$, then the state after measurement becomes
$$
|\Psi_{I}\rangle =|\Phi_{I}\rangle _{AA'} (\alpha|0\rangle _{B}|0\rangle _{C}+\beta|1\rangle _{B}|1\rangle _{C})
$$
That is, Bob and Charlie end up in the entangled state $|\Phi \rangle$ originally shared by Alice and Charlie!

If instead, Alice had found $\lambda_{X}$, then the state left over is
$$
|\Psi_{X}\rangle =|\Phi_{X}\rangle _{AA'}(\alpha|1\rangle _{B}|0\rangle _{C}+\beta|0\rangle _{B}|1\rangle _{C})
$$
which is not the entangled state $|\Phi \rangle$. But, if Bob measures $X$ on his state, then we get
$$
(X\otimes I)(\alpha|1\rangle |0\rangle +\beta|0\rangle |1\rangle )=\alpha|0\rangle |0\rangle +\beta|1\rangle |1\rangle =|\Phi \rangle 
$$
which *is* the entangled state we wanted. This is also true of $Y$ and $Z$. 

What is particularly special about this is that Bob and Charlie's qubits *never interacted*. We may imagine that Charlie prepared a state $|\Phi \rangle$ (by interacting two qubits together), before sending one to Alice. We may similarly assume that Bob prepared the state $|\Phi_{I}\rangle$ in a similar way, before sending a qubit to Alice. The qubits left in Charlie and Bob's possession were therefore *never* in the same place at the same time. Nevertheless, Alice can entangle them at a distance, using the teleportation protocol and classical communication. 

But how is this useful? Well, assume Alice, Bob and Charlie are all in a line, with Charlie 10km to the west of Alice, and Bob 10km to the east. Imagine that a qubit can be sent a maximum of 10km, otherwise it will be lost. 

![[quantum_repeater.excalidraw.png]]

However, using entanglement swapping, we can think of using Alice as a **quantum repeater** such that Charlie first distributes their entanglement to Alice, and then the protocol outlined above can distribute the entanglement a further 10km to Bob. 

Of course, Charlie could just send his qubit to Alice, who could then send it to Bob. This is different though. Here, we would like to establish the entanglement *ahead of time* - i.e. Charlie might not know what his state is in advance, but we can still establish the entanglement and then send the qubit afterwards, without having to go through this process after Charlie discovers the bit (where it might fail, and we have to do it again etc.).
