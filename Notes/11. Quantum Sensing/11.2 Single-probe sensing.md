In order to build some intuition, it will be constructive to consider the simplest possible scenario, or using a *single* qubit as a probe. Let's imagine that we prepare our probe in the state 

$$
|+\rangle =\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(|0\rangle +|1\rangle )
$$

Now consider the problem introduced in [[11.1 The sensing problem]]. Probing the magnetic field corresponds to applying the unitary operator $U_{\phi}$, in which case, our state becomes

$$
\begin{align}
|\psi_{\phi}\rangle  & = U_{\phi}|+\rangle  \\
 \\
 & = (e^{\frac{-i\phi}{2}}|0\rangle \langle  0|+e^{\frac{i\phi}{2}}|1 \rangle \langle 1|)\cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }} (|0\rangle +|1\rangle ) \\
 \\
 & = \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2 }}(e^{- \frac{i\phi}{2}}|0\rangle +e^{\frac{i\phi}{2}}|1\rangle )
\end{align}
$$

We now want to measure the state, and we can use $X=|+\rangle \langle+|-|-\rangle \langle-|$ to do so. In this case, the probabilities are

$$
P(+1)=|||+\rangle \langle +|\psi_{\phi}\rangle ||^{2}=\bigg| \frac{e^{-\frac{i\phi}{2}}+e^{\frac{i\phi}{2}}}{2}\bigg|^{2}=\cos^{2} \frac{\phi}{2}
$$

and 

$$
P(-1) = |||-\rangle \langle -|\psi_{\phi}\rangle ||^{2}=\bigg | \frac{e^{\frac{-i\phi}{2}}-e^{\frac{i\phi}{2}}}{2}\bigg|^{2}=\sin^{2} \frac{\phi}{2}
$$

The expectation value is then

$$
\langle X\rangle _{\phi} = \cos^{2} \frac{\phi}{2} - \sin^{2} \frac{\phi}{2}= \cos \phi
$$

So, we could use the statistical outcomes of the measurement to estimate $\phi$.

The interesting question is 'what is the resolution?', that is, how accurately can we hope to determine $\phi$. This will depend on how sensitive we are to variations in $\phi$. 

The answer is that we will *always* have some uncertainty in $\langle X\rangle_{\phi}$ since we cannot measure infinite times. We therefore need to understand how this translates into uncertainty in $\phi$. In order to resolve two distinct values of $\phi$, they need to be separated by *more* than the uncertainty we have in $\phi$. 

Assuming that $\phi$ takes on the particular value $\phi_{0}$, if the estimated expectation value is $x_{0}$ with uncertainty $\Delta x_{0}$, then the uncertainty $\Delta \phi_{0}$ in the value $\phi_{0}=\arccos(x_{0})$ is

$$
\Delta \phi_{0} = \frac{\Delta x_{0}}{\bigg | \frac{d \langle X\rangle _{\phi}}{d\phi} \Big |_{\phi=\phi_{0}}\bigg |}
$$

i.e. it is precisely the uncertainty in $x_{0}$, rescaled by the rate of change of the expectation value at the estimated point. This is a standard result from statistics, and is found by taking a Taylor expansion of $\langle X \rangle_{\phi}$ around $\phi_{0}$.

The final question to ask is 'what determines $\Delta x_{0}$?'. As we said above, it arises due to finite statistics (we cannot measure infinitely many times), but more formally, we know that it is the *standard deviation* that sets the overall scale of the uncertainty. In particular, we can actually work this out. Recall that the standard deviation $\sigma$ is given by

$$\sigma = \sqrt{ \langle D^{2} \rangle-\langle D\rangle^{2} }$$
for some distribution $D$, and so in our case, we can find

$$
\langle X^{2}\rangle _{\phi}=(+1)^{2} P(+1)+(-1)^{2}P(-1) = \cos^{2} \frac{\phi}{2} + \sin^{2} \frac{\phi}{2}
$$

and from above $\langle X\rangle_{\phi}^{2}=\cos^{2} \frac{\phi}{2}$ and so

$$
\Delta X_{\phi} = \sqrt{ \langle X^{2} \rangle - \langle X \rangle ^{2} }= \sqrt{ 1-\cos^{2} \frac{\phi}{2} } = |\sin \phi|
$$

After $N$ repetitions, assuming again $\phi=\phi_{0}$ in every case, the uncertainty $\Delta x_{0}$ is

$$
\Delta x_{0} = \frac{\Delta X_{\phi_{0}}}{\sqrt{ N }}
$$

That is, our uncertainty decreases as $\frac{1}{\sqrt{ N }}$ with the standard deviation $\Delta X$ setting the overall scale. This is just the *standard error on the mean* from statistics. 

Putting everything together, we find 

$$
\Delta \phi_{0} = \frac{\frac{|\sin\phi_{0}|}{\sqrt{ N }}}{|-\sin \phi_{0}|}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ N }}
$$

This shows us that in this case, in order to increase resolution, we must send through more probe states. We would need to send in four probe states to double the resolution, and so on. This gets expensive.

The main advantage of quantum sensing is that there are much more efficient quantum probes. Some exist whereby to increase the resolution by 2, we only need to send in double the probes.
